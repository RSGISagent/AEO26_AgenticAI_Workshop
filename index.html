What you'll learn

A practical, hands-on introduction to agentic AI for remote sensing, GIS and Earth observation — taught entirely with Australian environmental case studies and open data. You'll leave having actually driven an AI agent, not just watched one.

🏄

Vibe coding

Describe the outcome in plain language and let the model write, run and fix the code. Build a working geospatial analysis from a prompt — no coding experience required.

🛠️

Agentic tool use

Give the model tools — search, code execution, files, APIs — and let it decide when to use them. Chain tools to turn open satellite and environmental data into maps and answers.

🤖

Autonomous agents

Set a goal, walk away, verify what the agent did — safely, inside a sandbox. Run a browser-based autonomous agent on a real remote-sensing task, then audit its work.

Case studies draw on open Australian data — Digital Earth Australia, Copernicus/Sentinel, Geoscience Australia, TERN and the Atlas of Living Australia — across vegetation, biodiversity, land cover and disaster resilience. The session also includes a segment on running open-weights models on local hardware: sovereign AI you can hold in your hand.

Presenters: Adam Roff (Senior Research Scientist, Vegetation & Biodiversity Mapping, DCCEEW NSW) and Fang Yuan (Earth Observation Australia), with guest speakers to be announced. Program details are draft and subject to change until the official AEO26 program is published.

2 · Bring a browser, or install the mobile app

Any modern browser on a laptop works — just log in at claude.ai. Or install the official app:

🍎 Claude for iOS / iPadOS  ·  🤖 Claude for Android  ·  all platforms at claude.com/download

⚠️ Please don't use the Claude Desktop app for this workshop

The desktop app is powerful precisely because it works directly on your machine: it reads and modifies local files, and its agentic modes act with the same permissions as your own user account. That's wonderful on a dedicated machine — and risky on the laptop that holds your email, credentials and department documents, especially while you're learning what autonomous agents do.

Use it only on a dedicated or disposable machine, or if you have experience sandboxing autonomous agents (containers, VMs, permission modes — see Anthropic's guide to sandbox environments). Everything in this workshop runs in the browser or mobile app, where the agent operates inside Anthropic's sandbox, not on your machine. That boundary is one of the things we'll be teaching.
